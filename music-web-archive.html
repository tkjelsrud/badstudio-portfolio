Archive • 2009-2014

Music Web Archive

This page condenses an older personal music site that ran as a rough sketchbook for demos, experiments and work-in-progress notes. The original archive spans 2009 to 2014 and mostly lived as a hand-edited table of MP3 bounces, Flash players and short production comments.

Rather than reproducing the whole thing row by row, this version keeps the useful parts: the timeline, the change in tools and a few representative tracks from different stages. It moves from game-mood sketches and MPC loop ideas into Elektron/Reaper sessions and more structured ambient work.

Selected pieces

MOODGAME1

05.09.2009 • Game idea session

One of the earliest entries in the archive. The note simply says "Mood and weather (outside)", which fits the whole period: short atmospheric sketches built around scene-setting rather than finished arrangement.

Continuations

07.10.2013 • Second draft

By 2013 the notes get more structural: second draft, improved form, still unfinished. This is a good midpoint in the archive, where sketches start sounding less like isolated loops and more like pieces being revised.

Atmosphere FM

18.02.2014 • Reaper sessions

A later work-in-progress note mentions EQ changes after a session with Lasse: a 1 kHz cut plus high and low shelving, still unfinished. That kind of small engineering note says a lot about the archive as a whole: it documented decisions in motion, not just finished tracks.

Timeline

  • 2009-2010: game-mood sketches, MPC tracks, sample-based ideas, and a few early YouTube-era uploads.
  • 2012: "new stuff" entries built around Machinedrum and Blofeld, still clearly sketch-oriented but more focused.
  • 2013: a dense period of song ideas, rehearsal recordings, Monomachine/Machinedrum studies and more deliberate arrangement notes.
  • 2014: Reaper-based sessions where mixing and revision notes become part of the archive itself.

Original context

The source page was titled tkjelsrud music and functioned more like a working notebook than a release page. It preserved old naming habits, blunt self-critique, gear references, and version-by-version bounces. That roughness is part of the point. It shows process before branding, and documents how the sound moved from fragmentary loop sketches toward the later Notoms/Bad Studio direction.
